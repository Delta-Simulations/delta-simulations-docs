# Cabin Heating and Ventilation

Several controls can affect airflow in a UH-60M cockpit. The **HEATER** changes the temperature of the air sent through the ducts. The **VENT BLOWER** circulates ambient air. Adjustable nozzles near the consoles let the pilots direct ram air where they want it.

## Heat source and airflow

The basic heating system mixes outside air with hot compressed air taken from an engine compressor. The latter is called *bleed air*. The APU can also supply it on the ground. The mixing system regulates the temperature delivered to the cabin and cockpit outlets. By contrast, the electric ventilation blower moves ambient air through the cabin ducts even when no heat is selected.

The **AIR SCE HT/STRT** label in Delta's [Before Start checklist](../../Procedures/Before_Start.md) means *air source for heat and start*. The selector identifies the pneumatic source. The separate heater control sets the heat delivered to the occupants. This explains why the air-source selector also appears in the add-on's [engine runup checklist](../../Procedures/Starting%20Engines.md).

## Cabin equipment varies

The manual describes more than one cabin installation. An **AUX CABIN HEATER** panel refers to supplemental cabin heat. The environmental control system (**ECS**) described for a medical cabin recirculates air and can cool it using an electrically driven refrigeration system. A panel near the rear medic seat controls that installation.

The documented simulator interaction is the air-source selector in the linked checklists. The auxiliary heater, rear-cabin ECS and temperature response depend on what the selected add-on variant implements.
