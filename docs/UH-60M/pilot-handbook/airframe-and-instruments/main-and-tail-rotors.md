# Main and Tail Rotors

Two details to look for in an exterior view are the four blades above the cabin and the angle of the tail rotor. On the UH-60M, both rotors receive power from the transmission, and each changes blade pitch in response to the flight controls.

## Main rotor head

The four main blades meet at a central hub. **Pitch** is the angle of a blade about its long axis. The swashplate and pitch links change that angle as the blades turn. The hub also lets each blade move up or down (*flap*) and move slightly forward or back in the plane of rotation (*lead* or *lag*). These are different motions from changing blade pitch.

When the rotor is stationary, droop stops support the blades. This accounts for the difference in blade position between a parked aircraft and one carrying its weight on the rotor. The manual also describes a vibration absorber at the rotor head that limits the vibration passed into the fuselage.

## Tail rotor

The tail rotor sits on the right side of the raised tail pylon. The tilt is intentional. Most of its thrust acts sideways to counter the turning effect of the main rotor and control yaw. Because its thrust is angled, a part of it also supports the helicopter's weight in a hover.

## Vibration in the cockpit

The airframe has a separate active vibration control system. It senses rotor-induced vibration in the aircraft and generates an opposing vibration. The **VIB CONT** switch in Delta's [Before Start checklist](../../Procedures/Before_Start.md) refers to this system. The checklist identifies the cockpit control. Camera shake or force feedback alone cannot tell you how much of the system your add-on models.

*Manual reference: TM 1-1520-280-10, Chapter 2, Section VIII, paragraphs 2-45 through 2-47, and Section IX, paragraph 2-54.*
