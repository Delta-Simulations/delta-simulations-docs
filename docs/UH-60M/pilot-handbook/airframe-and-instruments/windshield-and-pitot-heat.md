# Windshield and Pitot Heat

The windshield wipers, heated glass and heated pitot probes serve three different purposes. A wiper clears water that is already on the glass. Windshield heat acts on the glass itself. Pitot heat acts on the external probes that supply pressure information to the flight instruments.

| Cockpit label in the aircraft manual | What it refers to |
| --- | --- |
| **WINDSHIELD WIPER** | One motor drives a wiper on each pilot's windshield. **LOW** and **HI** select wiping speeds. **OFF** stops the blades where they are. **PARK** returns them to the inboard windshield frames. |
| **WINDSHIELD ANTI-ICE** | Transparent electrical conductors heat the glass for anti-icing and defogging. The pilot, copilot and center panes have individual switches. The center pane can therefore have its own heat control even though it has no wiper. |
| **PITOT HEAT** | Heating elements in the external pitot probes help keep their pressure openings clear of ice. The two probes sit behind and above the cockpit doors. |

A moving wiper is a visible mechanical effect. Pitot heat has no comparable effect on the windshield. If you're looking around the virtual cockpit, the separate controls explain why a clear windshield tells you little about what the probes are sensing. For the instruments that use those pressure inputs, see [Standby Flight Instruments](standby-flight-instruments.md).

These labels and switch positions describe the aircraft in the manual. The add-on's published cockpit checklists do not establish which glass-heating, wiper-parking or probe-icing effects are simulated.

*Manual reference: TM 1-1520-280-10, Chapter 2, Section IX, paragraphs 2-49 and 2-50, and Section XIV, paragraph 2-69.*
