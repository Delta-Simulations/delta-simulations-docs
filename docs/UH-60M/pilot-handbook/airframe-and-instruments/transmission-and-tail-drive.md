# Transmission and Tail Drive

The UH-60M has two engines and one main rotor. Each engine feeds its own input module on the main transmission above the cabin. The main transmission brings the two inputs together, reduces their speed and turns the main rotor mast. A second drive takes power aft to the tail rotor.

## Follow the power through the aircraft

| Component | What it drives |
| --- | --- |
| Main transmission | The main rotor above the cabin and the drive shaft leading into the tail. |
| Accessory modules | A generator and a hydraulic pump on each side. This mechanical connection helps explain why generator indications change as the rotor comes up to speed. |
| Intermediate gearbox | The next section of the tail drive at the bend near the base of the tail pylon. |
| Tail gearbox | The tail rotor at the top of the pylon. Its gears turn the drive through the final change in direction. |

## Read the indications

In the add-on's [engine runup checklist](../../Procedures/Starting%20Engines.md), **Q1** and **Q2** refer to the torque supplied by each engine. **NR** refers to the speed of their shared main rotor. Two torque indications and one rotor speed are what you would expect from this arrangement.

**XMSN** on a cockpit label stands for *transmission*. **MAIN XMSN** labels refer to the main gearbox oil. Engine oil belongs to a separate system. On EICAS, the **MAIN XMSN PRESS** message follows modeled rotor speed. See [crew alerts and test indications](../flight-displays/crew-alerts-and-test-indications.md) for the inputs behind the cockpit messages.
