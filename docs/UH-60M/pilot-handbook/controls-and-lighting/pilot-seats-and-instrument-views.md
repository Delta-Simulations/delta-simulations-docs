# Pilot Seats and Instrument Views

The H-60 includes dedicated cockpit views for the primary flight display, multifunction display, overhead panel and pedestal. Use an instrument view when you need to read a small label or select a switch. Return to the pilot view for the normal windshield sightline.

## Choosing a view

| View | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Pilot** | The standard forward view from the pilot seat. Use it as your reference after looking around. |
| **Landing pilot view** | A higher eye position angled down through the front of the cockpit. Useful for seeing more of the ground in front of the aircraft. |
| **Copilot** | The opposite seat. Useful for checking the other utility light or viewing a panel from the other side. |
| **PFD** | A close view of the primary flight display. |
| **MFD** | A close view of the multifunction display. |
| **Overhead** | The panel above the pilots. The preset aims upward and leaves your hands free to operate switches with the mouse. |
| **Pedestal** | The console between the seats. The preset looks down towards the controls and panel lettering. |

A camera change only changes your viewpoint. Selecting the **MFD** view, for example, moves your eye in front of that display. Use the display's bezel keys to select the page you want to read. **Overhead** and **Pedestal** are instrument views too, even though they point at switches.

## Quick looks and cockpit access

The cockpit includes three quick looks to the left and three to the right, plus up and down views. A dedicated quick-look hat lets you glance towards a side window without slewing a free camera back into position. If you also assign a hat to the searchlight, check for conflicts so that moving the lamp doesn't turn your head at the same time.

The aircraft tablet has a click point labeled **EFB HIDE/SHOW**. Use it if the tablet obstructs your view, and use the corresponding mount click point to bring it back. A separate **Pilot VR** viewpoint provides the headset starting position. If you are adjusting an eye position on a monitor, select the ordinary pilot viewpoint before judging the result.
