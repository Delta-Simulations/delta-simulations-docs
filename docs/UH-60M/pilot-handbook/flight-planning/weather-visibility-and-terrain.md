# Weather, Visibility and Terrain

For a useful comparison in MSFS, save the aircraft variant, departure position, time of day, gross weight and starting fuel. Change one weather setting for each flight. A change in both load and wind makes it difficult to tell why the second approach felt different.

| Change in the simulator | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| Headwind or tailwind along the route | Compare indicated airspeed with groundspeed on each leg. Recalculate arrival time using the groundspeed for that direction. |
| Warmer air at the same airport | Compare the power required for the same hover height and gross weight. Record field elevation and temperature with the reading. |
| Rain or reduced visibility | Check whether the windshield is limiting the view or the scenery itself is obscured. The cockpit wipers and the simulator weather visibility affect different parts of the picture. |
| Rising terrain beneath a level route | Watch the change in radar altitude while indicated barometric altitude and vertical speed remain nearly steady. |
| A night departure | Set lettering, flood and display brightness separately. Very bright instruments can make an unlit pad harder to see on the monitor. |

## Elevated-airport example

Suppose a simulator airport sits around 4,000 ft above sea level. With the local pressure setting, a barometric indication near 4,000 ft on the apron is reasonable. **RA** reads height over the surface. A low hover there still gives a small RA. A ground effect that depends on height over terrain should be judged against that second reading.

For comparison, repeat a short circuit from the same stand in daylight using a clear preset. Keep the collective position or power reading, weight, temperature and approximate hover height in your notes. Then try a warmer preset. Your result describes the installed simulator build under those conditions. Use [hover power and density altitude](hover-power-and-density-altitude.md) for a repeatable worksheet.

On a flight in poor simulated visibility, a blank **ICE DETECTED** line on EICAS gives no assurance about ice. This entry is inactive in V5.2.4. For the controls and readouts that are available, see [windshield and pitot heat](../airframe-and-instruments/windshield-and-pitot-heat.md) and [crew alerts](../flight-displays/crew-alerts-and-test-indications.md).
